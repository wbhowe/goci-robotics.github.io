Foundational Research

Understanding the principles behind autonomous systems.

Our research examines the mathematical, computational, and physical principles that determine how autonomous agents sense, communicate, coordinate, and respond to their environments.

01

Collective Dynamics

Investigating how local interactions generate information propagation, coordinated motion, and emergent collective behavior across large multi-agent systems.

02

Distributed Control

Developing and analyzing control architectures for networked autonomous systems operating with asymmetric interactions, communication constraints, delays, and incomplete information.

03

Bio-Inspired Autonomy

Studying mechanisms underlying collective biological behavior and translating those principles into models and control strategies for engineered robotic systems.

04

Autonomous Decision-Making

Exploring planning and decision-making methods that allow robotic systems to operate safely and effectively under uncertainty and changing environmental conditions.

05

Safety-Critical Control

Developing control and planning approaches that combine autonomous performance with explicit safety, stability, and operational constraints.

06

Information Propagation

Studying how information travels through networked robotic and biological systems and how network structure influences propagation speed, distortion, stability, and collective response.

Research Approach

From fundamental principles to robotic capability.

GOCI Robotics approaches autonomy as both a fundamental research problem and an engineering challenge. Understanding the mechanisms that govern stability, information flow, decision-making, and collective behavior can provide new approaches to designing autonomous systems that operate beyond the limitations of conventional centralized architectures.

Our work combines mathematical modeling, control theory, numerical simulation, robotic experimentation, and engineering design. This allows fundamental questions about autonomous and networked systems to be investigated while maintaining a pathway toward practical robotic applications.

Founder

William Howe

Founder & Principal Investigator

William Howe is the founder and Principal Investigator of GOCI Robotics, LLC and a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Washington. His current research focuses on robotics, autonomous systems, control theory, and multi-agent coordination, including the fundamental mechanisms governing information propagation and collective behavior in networked robotic systems.

Prior to his doctoral research, William accumulated more than a decade of engineering experience across the design, prototyping, testing, manufacturing, integration, and operation of complex engineered systems. His industry experience includes electromechanical systems, control and automation, system validation, and aerospace engineering, and culminated in leading the Mechanical Systems Design team for Blue Origin's New Shepard launch vehicle through its first human flight.

This combination of research and industry experience shapes GOCI Robotics' approach: investigate fundamental questions in autonomy and control while maintaining a direct connection to the engineering realities required to transition new ideas into reliable robotic systems.
